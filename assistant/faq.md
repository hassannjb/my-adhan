## How many daily prayers are there?
id: five-daily
asks: how many times a day do muslims pray | what are the five prayers | how many fard prayers are there | how many obligatory prayers | how many rakats in each prayer | how many rakat is fajr | how many rakat is dhuhr | how many rakat is asr | how many rakat is maghrib | how many rakat is isha | names of the five daily prayers
sources: Bukhari 46; Bukhari 349

There are five obligatory (fard) prayers every day and night. All Muslims agree on this (ijma').

- **Fajr**: 2 rak'ahs
- **Dhuhr**: 4 rak'ahs
- **Asr**: 4 rak'ahs
- **Maghrib**: 3 rak'ahs
- **Isha**: 4 rak'ahs

That is 17 obligatory rak'ahs a day. On Friday, the Jumu'ah prayer (2 rak'ahs after a sermon) takes the place of Dhuhr for those it is required of.

The five prayers were made obligatory on the night of the Prophet's ascension (al-Isra' wal-Mi'raj).

## What other prayers are there besides the five?
id: voluntary-overview
asks: what are the sunnah prayers | what are the nafl prayers | types of voluntary prayer | what prayers are not fard | list of sunnah and nafl salah | extra prayers
sources: Muslim 728; Bukhari 1180; Abu Dawud 1416; Muslim 1163

Besides the five obligatory prayers, the main ones are:

- **Witr**: an odd-numbered prayer at night after Isha. Hanafis hold it is wajib (necessary). The Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali schools hold it is a confirmed sunnah.
- **Sunnah rawatib**: the regular sunnah prayers before and after the obligatory ones.
- **Qiyam al-Layl / Tahajjud**: voluntary prayer at night, best in the last third of the night.
- **Duha**: a forenoon prayer between sunrise and midday.
- **Tarawih**: the night prayer of Ramadan.
- **The two Eid prayers**: the schools differ on their ruling (see the Eid answer).

There are also prayers tied to a reason, such as greeting the mosque (tahiyyat al-masjid), istikharah, and the eclipse prayer.

Ask about any of these for details.

## What are the sunnah prayers before and after the fard prayers?
id: sunnah-rawatib
prayers: fajr, dhuhr, maghrib, isha
asks: what are the sunnah rawatib | how many sunnah before dhuhr | how many sunnah after isha | sunnah of fajr | sunnah muakkadah | 12 rakats sunnah | how many sunnah rakats are there | is there sunnah before asr | sunnah after maghrib
sources: Tirmidhi 415; Muslim 728; Bukhari 1180; Muslim 725

All agree these are sunnah, not obligatory, and that the two rak'ahs before Fajr are the most emphasized of them ("better than this world and what it contains", Muslim 725).

Two narrations give the regular set:

- **Twelve rak'ahs** (Umm Habibah): 2 before Fajr, 4 before Dhuhr, 2 after Dhuhr, 2 after Maghrib, 2 after Isha.
- **Ten rak'ahs** (Ibn Umar): the same, but 2 before Dhuhr instead of 4.

How the schools treat them:

- **Hanafi**: the twelve are the confirmed (mu'akkadah) sunnah.
- **Shafi'i and Hanbali**: the ten are the confirmed sunnah. The extra two before Dhuhr are recommended.
- **Maliki**: no fixed number is tied to the prayers. Voluntary prayer before and after them is recommended, and the two rak'ahs before Fajr (al-raghibah) have special emphasis.

## Is Witr obligatory and how is it prayed?
id: witr
prayers: isha
asks: what is witr | is witr wajib | is witr fard or sunnah | how many rakats is witr | when is witr prayed | witr time | can i pray witr with one rakat | how to pray witr
sources: Abu Dawud 1416; Abu Dawud 1422; Bukhari 998; Bukhari 990

**Time** (agreed): after Isha until true dawn. It is best as the last prayer of the night (Bukhari 998).

**Ruling**:

- **Hanafi** (Abu Hanifa): wajib, a level below fard but above sunnah. Leaving it is sinful.
- **Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali** (and Abu Hanifa's students Abu Yusuf and Muhammad): a confirmed sunnah.

**Number of rak'ahs**:

- **Hanafi**: 3 rak'ahs with one salam, with qunut before bowing in the third rak'ah.
- **Shafi'i and Hanbali**: at least 1 rak'ah and at most 11. Three is common, prayed either as 2 then 1, or 3 together.
- **Maliki**: 1 rak'ah, preceded by 2 (the shaf') with a salam between them.

## What is Tahajjud or Qiyam al-Layl?
id: tahajjud
asks: what is qiyam al layl | what is tahajjud | when is the best time for tahajjud | how many rakats is tahajjud | night prayer | when is tahajjud prayed | can i pray tahajjud without sleeping | qiyam ul lail
sources: Muslim 1163; Bukhari 1145; Bukhari 990; Bukhari 1147

Voluntary prayer at night is a confirmed sunnah. It is "the most excellent prayer after the obligatory ones" (Muslim 1163).

- **Time**: after Isha until true dawn. The best time is the last third of the night (Bukhari 1145).
- **Rak'ahs**: there is no fixed number. It is prayed two by two (Bukhari 990). The Prophet's usual practice was 11 rak'ahs including witr (Bukhari 1147).
- **The names**: qiyam al-layl covers any voluntary night prayer after Isha. Many scholars use tahajjud specifically for night prayer offered after having slept.

## What is the Duha prayer and when is it prayed?
id: duha
asks: what is salat al duha | when is duha time | how many rakats is duha | chasht prayer | ishraq prayer | forenoon prayer | is duha sunnah
sources: Bukhari 1178; Bukhari 1176; Muslim 748

Duha is a recommended forenoon prayer. The Prophet advised Abu Hurayrah never to leave it (Bukhari 1178).

- **Time**: from when the sun has risen about a spear's length (roughly 15 to 20 minutes after sunrise) until shortly before midday (zawal). It is best when the day has become hot (Muslim 748).
- **Rak'ahs**: at least 2. The Prophet prayed 8 on the day of the conquest of Makkah (Bukhari 1176). Scholars give the upper limit as 8 or 12, and some set no limit.

When it is prayed right at the start of its time, many call it Ishraq.

## What is Salat al-Awwabin?
id: awwabin
prayers: maghrib
asks: what is awwabin | awabin prayer | six rakats after maghrib | prayer of the penitent | is awwabin after maghrib
sources: Muslim 748; Tirmidhi 435

The name is used for two different prayers:

- **Duha**: in an authentic hadith, the Prophet called the forenoon prayer, prayed when the day has become hot, "the prayer of the awwabin" (Muslim 748).
- **Six rak'ahs after Maghrib**: a hadith promises the reward of twelve years of worship for six rak'ahs after Maghrib (Tirmidhi 435). It is graded weak (da'if). Many Hanafi and Shafi'i scholars still call voluntary prayer between Maghrib and Isha "awwabin", based on reports from the early generations.

Voluntary prayer between Maghrib and Isha is recommended in general.

## How many rak'ahs is Tarawih?
id: tarawih
prayers: isha
asks: what is tarawih | is tarawih 8 or 20 | how many rakats taraweeh | when is tarawih prayed | taraweeh | ramadan night prayer
sources: Bukhari 2010; Bukhari 2013

**Agreed**: Tarawih is a confirmed sunnah in Ramadan, prayed after Isha. Praying it in congregation was established under Umar (Bukhari 2010).

**Number of rak'ahs**:

- **20 rak'ahs**: the position of the Hanafi, Shafi'i and Hanbali schools, from the practice in Umar's time.
- **36 rak'ahs**: reported by Malik as the practice of the people of Madinah. Many Malikis also accept 20.
- **8 rak'ahs** (plus witr): held by others, from Aisha's report that the Prophet did not exceed 11 in Ramadan or at other times (Bukhari 2013).

Since night prayer has no fixed upper limit, many scholars treat this as a matter with room for each practice.

## What makes each prayer's time start and end?
id: times-overview
asks: what makes a salah start | what makes a salah end | when do prayer times start | how are prayer times determined | what determines the time of salah | when does each prayer start and end | prayer time windows | how do i know when a prayer time comes in
sources: Abu Dawud 393; Muslim 612; Muslim 614

Each prayer's time is set by the sun. The basis is the hadith of Jibril leading the Prophet in prayer at the start and end of each time over two days (Abu Dawud 393), and the Prophet's own description of the times (Muslim 612).

- **Fajr**: from true dawn (light spreading along the horizon) until sunrise.
- **Dhuhr**: from when the sun passes its highest point and starts to decline, until Asr begins.
- **Asr**: from when an object's shadow equals its length (twice its length for Abu Hanifa), until sunset. Delaying it until the sun yellows is disliked.
- **Maghrib**: from sunset until the twilight disappears.
- **Isha**: from when the twilight disappears until true dawn. Its preferred time ends at a third or a half of the night.

The schools differ on some of these boundaries. Ask about a specific prayer (for example "When does Maghrib end?") to see each view.

## When does Fajr start and end?
id: fajr-time
prayers: fajr
asks: when does fajr start | when does fajr end | when does fajr time finish | what is true dawn | what is false dawn | until when can i pray fajr | fajr time ends at sunrise | is it better to pray fajr early or late | subh time
sources: Abu Dawud 393; Muslim 612; Muslim 614; Bukhari 579; Tirmidhi 154

**Start** (agreed): true dawn (al-fajr al-sadiq). This is the light that spreads sideways along the horizon. It is not the "false dawn", a vertical band of light that comes before it.

**End** (agreed): sunrise (Muslim 612). Whoever catches one rak'ah before sunrise has caught the prayer (Bukhari 579).

**Best time**:

- **Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali**: early, while it is still dark (taghlis). This was the Prophet's regular practice.
- **Hanafi**: when the light has spread (isfar), based on "Pray Fajr at isfar, for its reward is greater" (Tirmidhi 154).

Apps estimate true dawn from the sun's angle below the horizon, which is why Fajr differs between apps (see "Why do apps show different Fajr and Isha times?").

## When does Dhuhr start and end?
id: dhuhr-time
prayers: dhuhr
asks: when does dhuhr start | when does zuhr end | when does zohar time finish | what is zawal | when does the sun decline | until when can i pray dhuhr | is it better to delay dhuhr in summer | noon prayer time
sources: Abu Dawud 393; Muslim 612; Bukhari 541; Bukhari 533

**Start** (agreed): zawal, when the sun has passed its highest point and begins to decline, so shadows start to lengthen again.

**End**: when Asr begins. The schools differ on when that is:

- **Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali**, and Abu Hanifa's students Abu Yusuf and Muhammad: when an object's shadow equals its length.
- **Abu Hanifa**: when the shadow is twice the object's length.

In both views, the shadow the object already had at zawal is not counted.

**Best time**: early in its time. In intense heat, delaying it a little until it cools is sunnah (Bukhari 533).

## When does Asr start and end?
id: asr-time
prayers: asr
asks: when does asr start | when does asr end | when does asar time finish | hanafi asr time | why is hanafi asr later | asr shadow length | until when can i pray asr | can i pray asr when the sun is yellow | what is the asr mithl
sources: Abu Dawud 393; Muslim 612; Muslim 614; Muslim 622; Bukhari 579

**Start**:

- **Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali**, and Abu Yusuf and Muhammad: when an object's shadow equals its length (plus its shadow at zawal). Apps call this "Standard" or "Shafi'i" Asr.
- **Abu Hanifa**: when the shadow is twice the object's length. Apps call this "Hanafi" Asr, and it comes later.

**End** (agreed): the prayer counts as on time until sunset. Whoever catches one rak'ah before sunset has caught it (Bukhari 579). Delaying it on purpose until the sun turns yellow is condemned as "the prayer of the hypocrite" (Muslim 622).

The schools describe the later part differently:

- **Hanafi**: valid until sunset. Delaying it until the sun yellows is disliked (makruh).
- **Shafi'i**: the preferred time lasts until the shadow is twice the object's length. After that it is valid without dislike until the sun yellows, and disliked after that.
- **Maliki and Hanbali**: the chosen time lasts until the sun yellows. From then until sunset is a time of necessity. Delaying into it without an excuse is sinful, though the prayer still counts.

The clock on this page uses the Standard (1x shadow) Asr.

## When does Maghrib start and end?
id: maghrib-time
prayers: maghrib
asks: when does maghrib start | when does maghrib end | how long is maghrib time | until when can i pray maghrib | what is shafaq | red twilight | white twilight | is maghrib time short | sunset prayer time
sources: Abu Dawud 393; Muslim 612; Muslim 614

**Start** (agreed): sunset, once the whole disc of the sun has gone below the horizon. Praying it promptly is sunnah.

**End**: the schools differ:

- **Hanbali**: when the red twilight (al-shafaq al-ahmar) disappears (Muslim 612).
- **Hanafi**: Abu Hanifa held it lasts until the white twilight (al-shafaq al-abyad) disappears, which is later. His students Abu Yusuf and Muhammad held the red twilight. Later Hanafi scholars gave rulings with both views.
- **Shafi'i**: al-Shafi'i's later ("new") view is that the time is short, only long enough after sunset for purification, adhan, iqamah and five rak'ahs. This is because Jibril led Maghrib at the same time on both days (Abu Dawud 393). His earlier view, which al-Nawawi and others judged stronger by the hadith, extends it until the red twilight disappears.
- **Maliki**: the chosen time is short, as in the Jibril hadith. A time of necessity extends with Isha until dawn.

## When does Isha start and end?
id: isha-time
prayers: isha
asks: when does isha start | when does isha end | until when can i pray isha | can i pray isha after midnight | is it better to delay isha | last time for isha | isha time finish | esha time
sources: Abu Dawud 393; Muslim 612; Muslim 614; Tirmidhi 167

**Start**: when the twilight has disappeared. This follows each school's view of when Maghrib ends: the red twilight for the majority, and the white twilight for Abu Hanifa.

**End**: all agree the prayer is not missed before true dawn. The schools differ on its chosen time:

- The Jibril hadith puts the end of its chosen time at a third of the night (Abu Dawud 393). Another hadith says "until half of the night" (Muslim 612).
- **Hanafi**: valid until true dawn. Delaying it past the middle of the night without need is disliked.
- **Shafi'i**: the preferred time is until a third of the night. After that it is still valid until true dawn.
- **Maliki and Hanbali**: the chosen time is until a third of the night (or the middle, in another view). After that until dawn is a time of necessity, and delaying into it without an excuse is sinful.

**Best time**: delaying it towards a third of the night is better if it is not hard on people (Tirmidhi 167). Many schools still prefer praying it early in congregation.

To get tonight's times, ask "When is midnight tonight?"

## When is it forbidden to pray?
id: forbidden-times
asks: forbidden prayer times | makruh times for salah | can i pray at sunrise | can i pray at sunset | can i pray after asr | can i pray after fajr | when is zawal | when is prayer not allowed | can i make up a missed prayer after asr | istiwa time
sources: Muslim 831; Bukhari 586

**Agreed**: there are times when voluntary prayer is prohibited:

1. After praying Fajr, until the sun has risen.
2. While the sun is rising, until it is about a spear's length up (roughly 15 to 20 minutes after sunrise).
3. When the sun is at its highest point (istiwa), until it starts to decline. These are the few minutes just before Dhuhr.
4. After praying Asr, until sunset.
5. While the sun is setting.

The first and fourth are tied to having prayed Fajr or Asr, not to the clock.

**What else is affected**:

- **Hanafi**: at the three times of rising, zenith and setting, no prayer is valid at all, not even a missed obligatory one. The exception is that day's Asr, if someone is late. After Fajr and Asr, only voluntary prayers are disliked, and missed prayers may be made up.
- **Shafi'i**: prayers with a cause are allowed at all these times, such as a missed prayer, the funeral prayer, greeting the mosque, or the two rak'ahs after wudu. Any prayer is allowed in the Haram of Makkah, and at zenith on Friday.
- **Maliki**: missed obligatory prayers may be made up at any time. In the well-known view of the school, the zenith is not a prohibited time.
- **Hanbali**: missed obligatory prayers may be made up at any time, as may the two rak'ahs of tawaf. Other voluntary prayers are not allowed. Some Hanbali scholars, such as Ibn Taymiyyah, allowed prayers with a cause.

## When is Jumu'ah prayed?
id: jumuah-time
prayers: dhuhr
asks: what time is jummah | when does jumuah start | friday prayer time | is jummah at dhuhr time | can jumuah be prayed before zawal | how many rakats is jummah
sources: Bukhari 904; Muslim 858

Jumu'ah is 2 rak'ahs after a sermon (khutbah), and it takes the place of Dhuhr. It is obligatory on adult, resident Muslim men who have no valid excuse.

**Time**:

- **Hanafi, Maliki and Shafi'i**: the time of Dhuhr. It starts after zawal, as the Prophet prayed it when the sun had declined (Bukhari 904).
- **Hanbali**: its time begins earlier, in the forenoon, with Muslim 858 among the evidence. Praying it after zawal is better and avoids the disagreement.

## When are the Eid prayers and are they obligatory?
id: eid
asks: eid prayer time | when is eid salah | is eid prayer wajib | is eid prayer fard | how many rakats is eid prayer | eid ul fitr prayer | eid ul adha prayer
sources: Abu Dawud 1135

**Time** (agreed): from when the sun has risen about a spear's length (roughly 15 to 20 minutes after sunrise) until zawal. The Companions disliked delaying it (Abu Dawud 1135).

**Ruling**:

- **Hanafi**: wajib.
- **Maliki and Shafi'i**: a confirmed sunnah.
- **Hanbali**: a communal obligation (fard kifayah).

It is 2 rak'ahs with extra takbirs, and the sermon comes after the prayer.

## Why do apps show different Fajr and Isha times?
id: calc-methods
prayers: fajr, isha
asks: what calculation method should i use | why is fajr different on different apps | what is isna method | what is 18 degrees | fajr angle | isha angle | what is muslim world league method | which method is correct | change calculation method
sources: Abu Dawud 393

Fajr is true dawn and Isha is the end of twilight. People can observe both, but a calculation has to estimate them from how far the sun is below the horizon. Different bodies chose different angles from their own observations:

- **ISNA (North America)**: Fajr 15°, Isha 15°
- **Muslim World League**: Fajr 18°, Isha 17°
- **Karachi**: Fajr 18°, Isha 18°
- **Egyptian**: Fajr 19.5°, Isha 17.5°
- **Umm al-Qura (Makkah)**: Fajr 18.5°, Isha 90 minutes after Maghrib

A larger angle gives an earlier Fajr and a later Isha. Dhuhr and Maghrib hardly change between methods, because they come from the sun's position directly. Asr differs by school (see the Asr answer).

To change the method on this page, use the gear menu, then Calculation Method. The chat uses the same setting as the clock.

## What is the Islamic midnight?
id: islamic-midnight
prayers: isha
asks: how is islamic midnight calculated | what is the middle of the night | what is the last third of the night | how do i find the last third of the night | when is the night divided into thirds
sources: Muslim 612; Bukhari 1145

In the rulings on prayer, the night is counted from sunset (Maghrib) to true dawn (Fajr), not from 6 PM to 6 AM.

- **Middle of the night**: halfway between Maghrib and Fajr. It matters for when Isha's chosen time ends (Muslim 612).
- **Last third of the night**: the final third of that span. It is the best time for night prayer and supplication (Bukhari 1145).

Some scholars count the night up to sunrise instead of Fajr, which moves these times slightly later.

To get tonight's times, ask "When is midnight tonight?" or "When does the last third of the night start?"
